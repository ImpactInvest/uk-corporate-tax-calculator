How this model works

Everything on the model page comes from one calculation, run in the order below. Nothing is estimated or smoothed: change an input and every figure downstream is recomputed from the statutory rates listed on this page.

  1. Client revenue, excluding VAT, less business expenses.
  2. Less director’s gross salary and the employer National Insurance on it.
  3. Less any employer pension contribution — deductible for the company, not taxable on you.
  4. The remainder is taxable profit. Corporation tax is applied with marginal relief where profit falls between £50,000 and £250,000.
  5. Profit after tax is distributable. Dividends are declared from it; whatever is left stays in the company.
  6. On your side: the personal allowance is set against salary and other income first, then dividends are treated as the top slice of income. Employee NI applies to salary only.
  7. Net in pocket is salary plus dividends, less the income tax, employee NI and dividend tax that arise because of them.
  8. If a Plan 2 student loan is switched on, 9% of repayment income above the threshold is deducted last. Salary is repayment income; dividends and other unearned income join it in full once they exceed £2,000 in the year.

Timing rules behind the calendar

The accounting period modelled here runs 1 November to 31 October, so company deadlines sit off the tax year.

Corporation tax — 1 August
Nine months and one day after the 31 October period end · gov.uk pay corporation tax
CT600 and accounts — 31 October
Twelve months after the period end for the return; accounts to Companies House nine months after
PAYE and NI — quarterly, 22nd
Quarterly payment is allowed while average monthly liability is under £1,500, which a minimum salary always is · gov.uk pay employers’ PAYE
Self assessment — 31 January
Balancing payment for the tax year just ended, plus the first payment on account for the next · gov.uk payments on account
Student loan — split collection
The share arising on salary is deducted through payroll each month; the share arising on dividends is settled with the 31 January self assessment balancing payment and feeds the payments on account.
Payments on account — 31 July
Each instalment is half the previous year’s liability that was not collected at source. The model approximates this from the current year’s dividend tax.

Modelling choices

Judgement calls where more than one treatment was defensible.

Salary drawn evenly. One twelfth a month, and dividends spread evenly too, so the monthly chart is smooth. Real dividends are lumpy and can be timed across 5 April.
Dividend tax attributed to you. Net in pocket subtracts only the tax caused by company income — tax on other income is excluded from the split.
Other income sits below dividends. Dividends are the top slice, which is how HMRC stacks them, so other income pushes them into higher bands.
No reserves brought forward. Distributable profit is this year’s only. If the company has retained earnings, real headroom is higher.
Student loan is not a tax. It is shown as its own deduction and comes off net in pocket, but it is left out of “total tax & NI” and the effective tax rate — it repays a debt rather than funding the state.
Pension treated as yours. Counted separately from take-home, not as tax — locked until 57 but untaxed on the way in.
Tax year rates, company year cash. Rates come from the selected tax year; the calendar shows the 1 Nov – 31 Oct company year. A period straddling 6 April blends two rate sets in reality.
